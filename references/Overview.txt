# Auris — Workflow and Feature Opportunity Overview

## 1. Product vision

Auris is a configurable environmental acoustic monitoring platform designed to bring together:

* High-quality scheduled audio recording
* Environmental-noise measurement
* Soundscape and ecoacoustic analysis
* Optional project-specific AI detection
* Remote device management
* Cloud-based review and validation
* Long-term data visualisation

The system should serve users who need reliable acoustic evidence rather than limiting itself to either wildlife recording or conventional noise monitoring.

Auris could support projects involving:

* Rare and cryptic species
* Invasive species
* Birds, frogs, bats and insects
* Urban and rural soundscapes
* Traffic, aircraft and construction noise
* Industrial and machinery monitoring
* Biosecurity and compliance-oriented surveys
* Long-term acoustic change detection

The central principle is that **raw audio remains the primary evidence**, while measurements, acoustic indices and AI detections help users locate, interpret and prioritise relevant recordings.

---

# 2. Core Auris workflow

## Step 1: Create a project

A user creates a monitoring project in the Auris dashboard and defines:

* Project name and description
* Monitoring objectives
* Deployment locations
* Recording schedule
* Environmental-noise requirements
* Acoustic indices to calculate
* Data-retention policy
* Synchronisation interval
* Optional target-species detector

Projects could use predefined profiles such as:

* General biodiversity monitoring
* Target frog survey
* Rare bird detection
* Urban noise monitoring
* Traffic-noise assessment
* Machinery monitoring
* Continuous event watch
* Custom research configuration

Profiles would provide sensible defaults while allowing experienced users to modify individual settings.

---

## Step 2: Configure recorders

Recorders are assigned to a project and configured either locally or remotely.

Local setup would be used for:

* Initial commissioning
* Device naming
* Project assignment
* Time and location setup
* Recording tests
* Microphone-level checks
* Connectivity tests
* Storage checks
* Starting and ending deployments
* Fault diagnosis in the field

Remote configuration would allow users to:

* Change recording schedules
* Change synchronisation intervals
* Update analysis settings
* Deploy new AI models
* Adjust detection thresholds
* Request diagnostic information
* Update firmware
* Review device status

Configuration changes should be versioned so users can see exactly which settings were active at any point in a deployment.

---

## Step 3: Scheduled monitoring

During normal operation, Auris wakes according to its monitoring schedule and performs the tasks that are due.

A typical cycle could be:

1. Wake at the scheduled time.
2. Record the requested audio period.
3. Calculate selected sound-level measurements.
4. Calculate selected soundscape indices.
5. Run an optional target detector.
6. Store the audio and associated metadata.
7. Upload summaries or selected recordings when synchronisation is due.
8. Return to its low-power state.

Recording and synchronisation schedules should remain independent.

For example:

* Record for one minute every ten minutes.
* Upload detections and health information every six hours.
* Upload complete audio only when requested or when a target is detected.

This allows Auris to monitor frequently without requiring continuous network activity.

# 3. Audio recording features

Auris should provide flexible scheduled and event-based recording.

## Recording options

Users could configure:

* Recording duration
* Recording interval
* Sample rate
* Bit depth
* Channel configuration
* File format
* Gain profile
* Day and night schedules
* Seasonal schedules
* Date-specific monitoring periods

More advanced scheduling could include:

* Different recording intervals at dawn and dusk
* Recording only during selected hours
* Increased effort during a defined survey window
* Reduced recording during poor weather
* Temporary intensive monitoring after a detection
* Scheduled control recordings retained regardless of AI results

## Audio metadata

Every recording should include or reference:

* Recorder identity
* Project and deployment
* Start and end time
* Location
* Recording configuration
* Microphone and gain profile
* Calibration information
* Firmware version
* Analysis profile
* AI model version
* Battery and storage status
* Environmental conditions when available

This ensures recordings remain interpretable and reproducible long after collection.

# 4. Environmental-noise monitoring

Auris should treat environmental-noise measurements as a separate but complementary data layer.

Potential measurements include:

* LAeq
* LCeq
* LZeq
* Fast and slow maximum levels
* Peak sound level
* Sound exposure level
* L10, L50, L90 and L95
* Octave or one-third-octave band levels
* Clipping percentage
* Noise floor
* Broadband and low-frequency energy

Long-term indicators could be calculated in the backend, including:

* Daytime level
* Evening level
* Night-time level
* Lden
* Daily and weekly exposure trends

These measurements would make Auris useful beyond ecology, including for:

* Traffic and aircraft monitoring
* Wind-turbine studies
* Construction monitoring
* Industrial noise
* Urban soundscape research
* Community noise studies
* Equipment and infrastructure monitoring

Unless the complete instrument is independently tested and certified, Auris should be described as a **calibrated environmental acoustic monitoring platform**, rather than claiming formal sound-level-meter compliance.

---

# 5. Calibration workflow

Calibration should be integrated into the Auris workflow rather than treated as an external technical task.

## Device calibration

Each completed recorder should have a stored calibration profile linking digital audio values to sound pressure level.

The profile should record:

* Calibration date
* Calibration level and frequency
* Recorder serial number
* Microphone and gain profile
* Measured sensitivity
* Calibration equipment
* Firmware version
* Environmental conditions
* Frequency-response correction profile

## Field calibration

Users should be able to perform calibration checks:

* Before deployment
* After deployment
* Following microphone replacement
* Following enclosure or membrane changes
* When unusual drift is detected

The dashboard could compare pre- and post-deployment results and flag unexpected changes that may indicate:

* Water ingress
* Port blockage
* Membrane contamination
* Microphone damage
* Gain changes
* Condensation
* Hardware deterioration

Calibration history should remain attached to the recorder and relevant deployments.

# 6. Soundscape and acoustic-index analysis

Auris could calculate acoustic indices directly after recording or later in the backend.

Initial indices could include:

* Acoustic Complexity Index
* Normalised Difference Soundscape Index
* Bioacoustic Index
* Temporal entropy
* Spectral entropy
* Combined acoustic entropy
* Acoustic Diversity Index
* Acoustic Evenness Index

These should be presented as **soundscape descriptors**, not direct biodiversity scores.

The dashboard should avoid statements such as:

> Biodiversity increased by 20%.

Instead, it could report:

> Acoustic complexity and high-frequency activity were higher than the recent baseline.

Indices should always be linked to a documented analysis profile containing:

* Audio sample rate
* Segment duration
* Frequency limits
* FFT settings
* Window function
* Overlap
* Threshold values
* Algorithm version

This is necessary because different calculation settings can produce substantially different results from the same recording.

## Complementary simple metrics

Auris should also calculate more easily interpretable measurements such as:

* RMS level
* Peak level
* Acoustic activity
* Frequency-band energy
* Spectral centroid
* Dominant frequency
* Occupied bandwidth
* Clipped-sample percentage
* Broadband-noise indicators

These may often provide clearer explanations than formal indices alone.

# 7. Optional edge AI detection

AI should be an optional project feature rather than a requirement for using Auris.

The strongest use case is not broad identification of every species. It is **project-specific detection of one or a small number of target sounds**.

Examples include:

* A rare bird
* A cryptic frog
* An invasive cane toad
* A threatened insect
* A defined bat-call group
* A chainsaw
* A gunshot
* A machinery fault
* A marine mammal call

## Detection workflow

A typical AI-assisted recording cycle would be:

1. Record an audio sample.
2. Divide it into short analysis windows.
3. Run the selected detector.
4. Log confidence scores and timestamps.
5. Retain audio containing likely detections.
6. Retain uncertain detections.
7. Retain a sample of negative recordings.
8. Upload candidates at the next synchronisation.
9. Validate them through the backend.

The edge model should act as a **candidate finder**, not the final ecological authority.

## Suggested model classes

Even when the user only wants a yes-or-no result, the training data should include:

* Target species
* Similar species
* Common false-positive sounds
* Rain, wind or insects
* General background

This improves the model’s ability to reject confusing sounds.

## Audio-retention policy

Auris should not automatically delete every recording without a detection.

A defensible retention policy would be:

* Always retain positive detections.
* Retain uncertain detections.
* Retain periodic control recordings.
* Retain a random sample of negative recordings.
* Retain additional recordings after model updates.
* Allow projects to retain all audio when storage permits.

This creates evidence for auditing the detector and discovering false negatives.

# 8. Auris Detector Builder

A major future dashboard opportunity is an integrated system for training project-specific models.

## User workflow

The user would:

1. Create a detector.
2. Add target recordings.
3. Add background recordings.
4. Add similar or confusing sounds.
5. Review and label audio clips.
6. Start a training job.
7. Review model performance.
8. Choose an operating threshold.
9. Deploy the model to selected recorders.
10. Validate field detections.
11. Retrain using newly validated examples.

The underlying training would run on backend infrastructure rather than on the recorder or inside the browser.

## Dataset tools

The dashboard could provide:

* Audio upload
* Spectrogram display
* Clip playback
* Time-region selection
* Target-present and target-absent labels
* Confuser labels
* Site and recorder metadata
* Duplicate detection
* Dataset balance warnings
* Dataset versioning

It should also warn users when a dataset lacks diversity, for example:

> Most positive examples come from one animal, one location or one recording session.

## Model evaluation

The dashboard should show more than overall accuracy.

Important metrics include:

* Recall
* Precision
* False positives per recording hour
* False negatives
* Confusion matrix
* Performance by site
* Performance by recorder
* Performance under rain or wind
* Performance at different thresholds
* Quantised-model performance
* Expected recorder memory and processing requirements

For rare-species monitoring, the default should generally favour high recall, with false positives removed through backend review.

---

# 9. Backend validation and review

Candidate detections should appear in a central review queue.

Each review item could include:

* Audio player
* Spectrogram
* Detection time
* Edge-model confidence
* Optional backend-model confidence
* Recorder and location
* Environmental conditions
* Nearby detections
* Previous detections from the same site
* Model version

Users could label each candidate as:

* Confirmed
* Rejected
* Uncertain
* Needs expert review

Validated results would contribute to:

* Project reporting
* Distribution maps
* Activity plots
* Model evaluation
* Future training datasets

Rejected high-confidence detections are especially valuable because they identify sounds the current model strongly misunderstands.

---

# 10. Model lifecycle and deployment

Models should be treated as versioned project assets.

A model record should include:

* Model name and version
* Target labels
* Training dataset version
* Training configuration
* Input sample rate
* Window length
* Feature extraction settings
* Detection thresholds
* Evaluation results
* Supported firmware versions
* File checksum
* Deployment history
* Validation status

Models could progress through stages such as:

* Experimental
* Field trial
* Validated
* Active
* Retired

Deployment should support:

* One recorder
* A selected group
* An entire project
* Pilot deployment
* Staged rollout
* Rollback to the previous model

A recorder should verify a model before activation and retain a working fallback version.

# 11. Connectivity and data transfer

Auris should support two complementary workflows.

## Local field connection

Used for:

* Initial setup
* Device discovery
* Recording tests
* Microphone checks
* Device health
* Time and location setup
* Diagnosing failed remote connections
* Starting and ending deployments
* Downloading selected files

Local access should work without mobile coverage or an internet connection.

## Remote platform connection

Used for:

* Health reporting
* Detection uploads
* Configuration updates
* Model deployment
* Firmware updates
* Remote schedule changes
* Battery and storage monitoring
* Project-wide management

The recorder should not require continuous connectivity. It can store data locally and synchronise according to the project schedule.

# 12. Storage and upload management

Auris should allow users to balance ecological value, storage capacity, bandwidth and power consumption.

Possible upload levels include:

* Health data only
* Detection metadata only
* Acoustic-index summaries
* Environmental-noise summaries
* Spectrogram thumbnails
* Short detection clips
* Complete positive recordings
* Complete scheduled recordings
* On-demand retrieval

The dashboard should clearly distinguish:

* Data stored on the recorder
* Data waiting for upload
* Data available in the cloud
* Data archived externally
* Data marked for deletion

Retention rules should be configurable by project and should never delete irreplaceable raw data without an explicit policy.

# 16. Key product principles

## Raw audio remains available

Derived measurements and AI detections must not replace the original evidence.

## AI remains optional

Auris should remain a capable recorder and environmental acoustic monitor when AI is disabled.

## Models are project-specific

A narrowly focused detector is often more useful and more realistic than a universal species classifier.

## Edge AI prioritises candidates

Final confirmation can involve larger backend models and human review.

## Analysis must be reproducible

Every measurement and detection should retain its settings, software version and calibration context.

## Local access remains available

Users need a way to commission and diagnose recorders even when remote connectivity fails.

## Power and bandwidth are managed through scheduling

Recording, inference and synchronisation should occur only as often as required by the monitoring objective.

# 18. Overall opportunity

Auris has the potential to occupy a useful space between simple autonomous recorders and expensive specialised monitoring systems.

Its strongest proposition is not merely:

> A recorder with AI.

It is:

> A configurable environmental acoustic platform that records reliable evidence, measures sound, detects project-specific targets, manages devices remotely and turns validated field recordings into continuously improving monitoring tools.

The combination of scheduled low-power recording, environmental-noise measurements, acoustic indices, optional target detection and backend validation would make Auris useful across ecology, conservation, environmental assessment and broader acoustic monitoring.
