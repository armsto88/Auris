  • Where this comes from: the panel input, charger, temperature cut-out, battery input and keep-alive rail are FieldMesh Node v4; the modem rail is FieldMesh Hub v2 r6; the wake latch is common to both. Decisions ADR-007, 008, 010, 011 and the owner's choices of 2026-10-23; review in doc 20. Designed and reviewed there, not yet built or field-proven.
  • Changed from FieldMesh: cold trip lowered from +5 °C to 0 °C (R209 21.5 kΩ), latch inverter resistors raised from 100 kΩ to 1 MΩ (about 5 µA instead of 46 µA), a USB wake input added, and no USB charging.
  • Sizing target: energy-neutral at 50–56°N in December with the 1S2P pack giving at least 10 days without sun. About 3 W of panel should do with the CN3791; confirm on the bench before choosing the panel.
  • Design load: at most 1.0 Wh/day; recording at most 350 mW at the battery (F4-06). Sleep current is about 140–180 µA, mostly the DS3231M itself.
  • Charger: the CN3791 holds the panel at its maximum-power voltage, set by R204/R205 to 6.05 V for the Node v4 panel. Recalculate them for the chosen panel. It has no power path and no safety timer, so an awake recorder delays charge termination.
  • Battery state: measured as voltage through the R216/R217 divider on VBAT_SW (decision 2026-10-23; the MAX17048 fuel gauge is dropped). State of charge is estimated from voltage before the modem turns on. The divider draws nothing while asleep.
  • USB: programming and service only. VBUS wakes the board but does not charge the battery.
  • Not on the LCSC list: the battery pack (FieldMesh supply) and the solar panel with its sealed cable.

MCU

{{comp:mcu}}
  • Module: 16 MB quad flash, 8 MB octal PSRAM, Wi-Fi and BLE on a PCB antenna. Supply 3.0–3.6 V.
  • Temperature: the R8 variants are rated −40 to +65 °C ambient, lower than other variants. The enclosure must keep its inside air at or below 65 °C in full sun (F7-01).
  • Antenna keep-out: keep copper and the LTE antenna away from the module's antenna end; it is used for the Wi-Fi setup hotspot.
  • Pin map: the full allocation is backlog item H0-06. The table below shows every GPIO the audio, power, LTE and storage sections use (proposed 2026-10-23). To fit the microSD bus, the UART0 console was given up: programming, console and debug all run over the built-in USB-Serial-JTAG on GPIO19/20.

RTC and wake

{{comp:rtc}}
Wake sourceHow it is setWhat the MCU does
Alarm 1 (A1F flag)One-shot, rewritten by the MCU before every power-downRun the next scheduled job, e.g. record the 10-minute slot
Alarm 2 (A2F flag)Standing daily match on hour and minute, UTC; never needs touchingDaily LTE session: heartbeat, config, clock sync
Wake button (SW201)Pulls the latch low directlyCommissioning, Wi-Fi hotspot, status LED. No alarm flag set means a button or USB wake
USB presentVBUS turns on Q209 through R225/R226USB service. USB does not charge the battery
  • Bus: I²C address 0x68 on the system bus (SYS_SDA/SYS_SCL, 4.7 kΩ pull-ups to 3V3), alone on it for now. The audio ADC has its own bus. The fuel gauge was dropped on 2026-10-23.
  • INT/SQW: active-low, open-drain, pulled up to the always-on rail and fed to the wake latch. Write INTCN = 1 and BBSQW = 0 at every boot so it never outputs a square wave.
  • Order of events at wake: the MCU asserts PWR_HOLD first, then reads and clears A1F/A2F. Clearing the flag first would drop the INT line and cut its own power.
  • Accuracy: ±5 ppm, about 0.43 s/day. The daily LTE sync corrects it (F2-02).
  • Supply current: up to 130 µA from VCC in standby, most of the board's sleep current. The VBAT pin runs only the 1 Hz square wave, not alarms, so VCC stays on the keep-alive rail. The CR1220 on VBAT keeps the time if the main battery runs flat.
  • Bench checks before schematic freeze (gate G1): alarm INT with the main rail off, A1 and A2 firing together, late or missing PWR_HOLD, brownout near VPF, and leakage into unpowered rails.
  • Full decision: docs/adrs/ADR-002-rtc-selection.md.

Mic 2 option (not fitted)

Mic 2 is a copy of mic 1 with different reference designators, wired to the ADC's spare input pairs. Place every footprint so a stereo or hydrophone version needs no new board. Leave the parts off for now.

Hydrophone build (proposed 2026-11-16): use a hydrophone with a built-in preamp, such as the CRT C57 or HTI-96-MIN. Its low-impedance output goes straight into VIN2 through the direct path; the OPA1662 is not used. Fit J102, FB103, C135, D102, R138, R139, C137, C138, R142, R143 and C139. Leave off the bias parts R140, R141 and C136, R137, and the Mic 2 preamp path.

These hydrophones need at least 5 V, which the board does not provide. Power them externally for now, for example from the maker's battery box, and keep J102 pin 2 for a later on-board supply. Check the hydrophone's output DC level first: D102 clamps at 5.5 V and the ADC pins take −1.7 to 5.0 V. Still open: a dual footprint at C137/C138 for 1 µF film, giving ≈ 8 Hz instead of ≈ 36 Hz for baleen whales.

Only 151 EG800K-EU modules were in stock at LCSC (179 at JLC) on {{snapdate}}. Order prototype quantity early.
  • Why the EG800K-EU (decided 2026-10-23): once GSM was ruled out, the A7670G's global GSM + LTE coverage was the only thing it offered over this module, at 2.5 times the price and twice the board area. The module has no 2G, so its supply never sees GSM's 2 A bursts. Sites with only 2G coverage get no LTE session (risk R19). For the Americas, the EG800K-LA fits the same footprint.
  • Source for the sheet below: Quectel's EG800K-LA Hardware Design V1.0.0 (2024-05-23, preliminary, from the FCC filing). The -LA and -EU are one series with one footprint; check the pin table against the EU manual when Quectel supplies it. The LGA pads sit under the module, so it needs JLC assembly or stencil and reflow, not hand soldering.
  • What it does in V1: one session a day. Heartbeat up (≤ 1 KB), configuration down, clock sync. No audio upload (decision D5). Budget ≤ 1 MB per month and ≤ 0.05 Wh per day.
  • Supply (ADR-011, decided 2026-10-23): TPS63020 buck-boost at 3.9 V from the switched rail, as on FieldMesh Hub v2: 3×22 µF plus 470 µF bulk, SMAJ5.0A clamp, enable soft-start and power-good to the MCU. Still measure LTE transmit at full power, low cell voltage and in the cold: about 1 A comes from the battery, close to the 1.1 A battery fuse's hold current when warm (risk R02).
  • Interface: main UART with RTS/CTS, STATUS and PWRKEY to the ESP32 through a level shifter (the module's I/O is 1.8 V). Firmware needs a Quectel AT driver; FieldMesh has only an A7670G one. The modem domain is switched off outside the sync window.
  • RF: U.FL connector to the FPC antenna. Keep the antenna feed and modem away from the microphone input and ADC. LTE transmit never overlaps a recording.
  • SIM: nano-SIM holder with ESD protection, drawn below. The SIM plan and operator acceptance per country (e.g. Telstra in Australia) are still open; a solder-down MFF2 SIM would remove the holder from a damp enclosure.
  • GNSS is not needed: site position comes from the phone at commissioning.

Storage and USB

{{comp:io}}
  • microSD bus (ADR-012, decided 2026-10-23): SD mode, 1-bit: SD_CLK GPIO47, SD_CMD GPIO48, SD_D0 GPIO44, routed through the ESP32-S3 GPIO matrix. About 1–2 MB/s, 10–20 times the 96 KB/s audio needs, on fewer pins than SPI. Pull CMD, D0 and the unused D1–D3 up to the card supply with 10 kΩ; D3 high keeps the card out of SPI mode.
  • Card supply: a load switch with an active-high enable on SD_PWR_EN (GPIO45) and a 100 kΩ pull-down, so the card is off until firmware turns it on and can be power-cycled if it hangs. The same pull-down holds GPIO45's strap at the 3.3 V flash setting during reset. Set the SD pins to inputs or low before switching the card off, or the card is powered through its pins. Switch part still to choose.
  • Status LED: on GPIO43 (formerly UART0 TX), wired to GND through a resistor. It flickers with the boot ROM log, which is harmless.
  • Card size: 128 GB lasts about 150 days and 256 GB about 300 days at 16-bit, 48 kHz, 1 minute in 10. Cards above 32 GB come formatted exFAT; firmware decides between exFAT support and FAT32.
  • Swap in the field: the card must be reachable without opening the main seal (F5-06). A power cut or card removal must lose at most the file being written (F3-02).
  • USB-C: native USB for programming and service only; it does not charge the battery (decision 2026-10-23). Needs 5.1 kΩ CC pull-downs, ESD protection and a VBUS sense line into the wake logic.
  • Copying data over USB is not practical: the ESP32-S3 USB is full-speed (12 Mbit/s), so 25 GB would take over 7 hours. Card swap is the way to collect audio.
