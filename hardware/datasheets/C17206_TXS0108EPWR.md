## Required external parts and equations

- 100 nF on each supply pin, close to the pin (§8.4.1, Fig. 8-3): C308 on VCCA, C309 on VCCB — OK.
- OE: "tie OE to GND through a pull-down resistor and do not enable OE until VCCA and VCCB are fully ramped" (§8.3; Fig. 8-3 caption); minimum pull-down set by the driver's sourcing ability. Control-input edge ≤ 10 ns/V (§5.3).
  - Auris OE before the 2026-11-17 changes: R304 100 kΩ from VDD_EXT, R305 1 MΩ to GND, C307 100 nF to GND. Since the changes C307 is not fitted (footprint kept, BOM fit = N), so OE = 0.91 × VDD_EXT with no delay, as in Quectel's divider below. The RC analysis that follows applies to the old circuit. Thevenin: V∞ = 1.8 × 1 M/1.1 M = **1.636 V**, R = 90.9 kΩ, τ = 9.09 ms. Time to reach VIH (1.17 V): t = τ · ln(1.636 / (1.636 − 1.17)) = **11.4 ms** after VDD_EXT (the README's "≈ 9 ms" is τ, not the crossing). Slope at the crossing: (1.636 − 1.17)/9.09 ms = 51 V/s, i.e. **≈ 19.5 ms/V against a 10 ns/V limit**. With the 1 MΩ divider the final level drops by 9 %, still ≥ 1.45 V with ±2 µA leakage — the divider itself is fine.
  - Power-down (with C307 fitted, before the 2026-11-17 changes): when VDD_EXT collapses, C307 holds OE near 1.6 V and discharges through R304 (into VDD_EXT, if the module pulls it low) and R305 (τ up to 100 ms if VDD_EXT goes high-impedance). OE then stays above 0.65·VCCA while VCCA falls — the opposite of §8.3. No damage (OE tolerates 5.5 V independent of VCCA, §5.3), but the part is nominally enabled while VCCA collapses.
  - Quectel's network (Ref V1.2 sheet 7; EG800K-LA HD Fig. 21): 10 kΩ from VDD_EXT, 120 kΩ to GND, **no capacitor** → OE = 0.92·VDD_EXT. A divider without a capacitor keeps OE at a fixed fraction of VCCA (0.91 with R304/R305), always above the 0.65·VCCA threshold once VCCA is valid and falling with it, so OE never crawls through its threshold. The module's UART is inactive for ≥ 10 s after VDD_EXT appears (EG800K-LA HD Fig. 11), so the absence of a delay costs nothing.
- Unused channels: SCES642L gives no rule for unused I/Os. With OE high the integrated pull-ups (§7.3.5) hold both sides of an open channel high; with OE low all I/Os are Hi-Z and the pull-ups are off. Leaving A6–A8/B6–B8 open is consistent with the datasheet. Quectel's Ref sheet 7 ties its unused channel (A1/B1) through 51 kΩ to GND on each side; against the 40 kΩ low-state pull-up that only produces a mid-level, so it is not a better choice.

### Is an auto-direction translator the right choice? (report only)

- **For it:** Quectel names this exact part (EG800K-LA HD §4.4; Ref sheet 7 note 1); one package covers all five lines; both sides are push-pull CMOS at low speed with short traces, the case where the TXS works well; the B port carries ±8 kV IEC contact ESD (§5.2); in stock (80 k at JLC).
- **Against:** auto-direction relies on one-shots and pull-ups whose interaction with external pulls needs checking (done above); B-port VIH/VIL limits (VCCI − 0.4 V / 0.15 V) are tighter than the ESP32's guaranteed output levels on paper; no explicit Ioff spec for the powered-off A side; TI itself says a TXB part may suit push-pull better (§8.1) — but a TXB0108 would be worse here: TXB parts have even weaker output buffers and TI's TXB guidance asks for external pull resistors above about 50 kΩ (general knowledge, TXB datasheet not read), while the ESP32's internal pulls are ≈ 45 kΩ.
- **Alternative:** every line has a fixed direction (2 MCU→modem: TX, RTS; 3 modem→MCU: RX, CTS, STATUS). Fixed-direction dual-supply buffers with VCC-isolation/Ioff (TI SN74AXC1T45 / SN74LVC1T45 / SN74LVC2T45 with DIR tied, or the fixed-direction TXU0x0x family, e.g. one 2+2 part plus one single) give hard push-pull drive and Schmitt-trigger inputs. Cost is similar or lower; the price is two packages instead of one and a non-Quectel design. **Verdict:** keep the TXS0108E for rev A (fix OE — done 2026-11-17, C307 not fitted); revisit only if bring-up shows UART glitches. Alternative part numbers here are from general knowledge, not checked against datasheets or stock.

## Layout notes and gotchas

- Short traces: the one-shots fire for about 30 ns; keep round-trip reflection delay below that and lumped load small (§7.3.3, §8.4.1). Place U302 between the module and the ESP32 with A-side traces short.
- Bypass capacitors at pins 2 and 19 (§8.4.1).
- Do not add external pull-downs < 50 kΩ-class on TXS lines without rechecking Eq. 1; do not add RC filters on the B side (the one-shot fights capacitance).
- Quectel's DNP 22/33 pF filter pads (Ref sheet 3 note 9; see the U301 file) were added on 2026-11-17 as C317–C323 (33 pF, not fitted); C318–C322 sit on the A-side lines (M_RXD, M_TXD, M_CTS, M_RTS, M_STATUS). If fitted they load the A side; 33 pF with the 4 kΩ high-state pull-up is still ≈ 130 ns — fine at 115 200 baud, but leave them unfitted unless RF pickup is seen.
